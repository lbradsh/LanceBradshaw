Research interests

I am interested in the social and behavioral consequences of conversational AI: how people evaluate its advice, assign responsibility when it makes mistakes, and use it to assess information. My current work focuses on two areas.

Trust & responsibility

AI & organizational accountability

I study how a chatbot’s humanlikeness and the wording of a company’s apology influence customer responses after a service failure. This work examines whom customers blame and whether they intend to pursue legal action against the company.

Guiding question

When AI makes a mistake, who do people hold responsible?

Information & judgment

AI & misinformation

I investigate whether interactive chatbots can help people evaluate misinformation in videos. In this work, a chatbot flags potentially false claims, explains why they were flagged, and answers participants’ follow-up questions.

Guiding question

Can a conversation with AI help people make better judgments about information?

02

Teaching

Before beginning my Ph.D., I spent two years as a lecturer at Indiana University, teaching courses in information systems and business technology.

My teaching emphasizes clear explanations, practical examples, and opportunities for students to work through problems themselves. I aim to create a classroom where students feel comfortable asking questions and connecting what they learn to real business challenges.
